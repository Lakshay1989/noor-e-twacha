## The logic
- **Positioned in the middle of the market, not the bottom.** The cheapest listings (₹169 to ₹300) are loss-leaders or
  unproven brands; we do not compete there. We sit at or just under the established D2C leaders (Minimalist, Derma Co).
- **Hero products are the three that map to the biggest search demand and the clearest story:** niacinamide serum
  (category's best-known active), Vitamin C serum (tan/dullness, huge Indian demand), SPF 50 (highest-frequency repurchase).
- **MRP:** each MRP is set to the category-standard MRP, and discounts stay at or below 35% (a test enforces this). Indian law
  requires the printed MRP on the pack, so **the MRP on the website must equal the MRP you print on the pack.** Do not show
  a discount from an MRP that is not on the pack.
- **We never claim to be cheaper than Amazon or any named brand.** We show only MRP, our price and the percentage between them.
- **Kits** are priced 15 to 16% below buying the four products separately at our own prices, so the saving is real and checkable.
- **UPI discount (5%)** replaces the need for blanket sale pricing and protects margin (no payment-gateway fee on UPI-to-account).

## Margin check you must do (I do not have your costs)
Per order: `selling price − product cost − packaging − shipping you pay − COD/payment cost − returns allowance`.
Typical D2C skincare: product + packaging at 15–25% of selling price. At ₹449 that is ₹70–110 landed cost, leaving
roughly ₹340 before shipping (₹60–90), COD handling and a returns allowance. If your landed cost is above ~₹130 for a
₹449 serum, raise the price or drop the UPI discount. Give me real quotes from the manufacturer and I will rebuild this table.
